How Much Does It Cost to Hire an SDR in 2026? Salary, Commission, and Global Hiring Costs

Build a realistic hiring budget by looking beyond base salary to incentives, recruiting, tools, training, management, and employment costs.

Hiring a sales development representative (SDR) can help a business generate leads, reach potential customers, and build a more predictable pipeline. But before hiring, business owners need to ask: how much does it actually cost to hire an SDR?

The answer depends on experience, location, compensation structure, industry, and responsibilities. A domestic hire and an international remote hire may have different pay and employment costs. Salary is only one part of the investment: recruitment, onboarding, tools, training, management, and commission all matter.

What is an SDR, and why do businesses hire one?

An SDR generally works at the start of the sales process: identifying potential customers, initiating conversations, and qualifying opportunities before handing them to an account executive or another sales professional.

Typical responsibilities include researching prospects, identifying decision-makers, writing personalized emails, making calls, following up, qualifying inbound leads, booking meetings, and maintaining CRM records. An SDR can give founders and senior salespeople more time for work that needs their expertise, but activity alone does not make the role profitable—the relevance and quality of opportunities matter.

How much does an SDR earn in 2026?

There is no single global SDR salary. Compensation differs by local market, role seniority, industry, sales cycle, language requirements, and whether the role focuses on inbound qualification or outbound prospecting. Use current, location-specific compensation data when setting a budget; do not treat US-dollar remote job listings or one survey as a global salary benchmark. When comparing remote SDR salary data, confirm whether the figures represent base pay or sales development representative compensation at target.

TermWhat it means
Base salaryFixed pay specified in the compensation agreement, subject to applicable law and terms.
CommissionVariable pay tied to clearly defined performance outcomes.
OTEOn-target earnings: base salary plus target variable pay if the stated target is achieved. OTE is not guaranteed income.

How to build an SDR compensation plan

Commission can be tied to qualified meetings, attended meetings, sales-qualified leads, opportunities accepted by account executives, or qualified pipeline. Reward meaningful outcomes rather than raw activity that could encourage low-quality bookings. Explain the calculation, eligibility rules, timing, and realistic target attainment before a candidate accepts.

What factors determine the cost of hiring an SDR?

1. Experience level

An entry-level candidate may need more training and supervision but can be a good fit when onboarding is structured. An experienced SDR may bring prospecting, CRM, objection-handling, and industry experience. Match experience to the complexity of your sales process.

2. Geographical location

Location can affect compensation expectations, recruitment availability, payroll, benefits, and administration. Compare the total employment cost, and do not assume geography determines quality or performance.

3. Industry and product complexity

Enterprise software, regulated products, and longer B2B sales cycles may call for industry knowledge, technical understanding, or experience with senior buyers. A simpler product may need a different training plan.

4. Language and target market

A role selling to English-speaking US buyers differs from one selling in German, French, Spanish, or Japanese. Specify the professional language ability and customer-market familiarity actually needed.

5. Working hours

Requiring an international hire to work a customer's local schedule can mean night or weekend work in the employee's location. State schedule expectations early and account for applicable working-time and compensation rules.

The hidden costs of hiring an SDR

A realistic budget includes more than salary and commission. Common cost categories include:

  • Recruitment: job advertising, sourcing, interview time, references, background checks where appropriate, and recruiter fees.
  • Sales tools: CRM, email, sales engagement, prospect research, scheduling, and call software. Buy only what the role needs.
  • Training and onboarding: product and industry education, personas, prospecting practice, objection handling, CRM instruction, and role-play.
  • Management: coaching, call and message review, performance conversations, territory planning, and process improvement.
  • Employment and administration: payroll taxes, required benefits, insurance, leave, local administration, international payment fees, and employer-of-record services where relevant.

What Reddit discussions can reveal about SDR compensation

Sales communities discuss fixed salary, hourly pay, commissions, inbound versus outbound responsibilities, and whether targets are achievable. These posts can give hiring managers context about the questions candidates may ask, but they are individual experiences—not a representative compensation survey.

  • Search r/salesdevelopment for SDR compensation discussions.
  • Search for discussions about SDR compensation plans.
  • Search r/sales for SDR OTE and salary conversations.

Use these conversations as prompts for designing a transparent plan: avoid paying for activity alone, make the commission formula understandable, align pay with responsibilities, and give representatives a reasonable opportunity to reach targets through sound territories, enablement, and coaching.

Is hiring an international SDR cheaper?

International recruitment may give a business more flexibility and access to a wider talent pool, including candidates in the Philippines, Nigeria, South Africa, India, Colombia, Kenya, Romania, or Poland. But an international hire is not automatically cheaper once the full cost is considered. If you plan to hire international SDRs, include recruitment and administration in your SDR recruitment cost estimate, not just the monthly pay.

Compare base pay, commission, recruitment, local employment obligations, payment arrangements, equipment, software, training, working hours, and management. Classification rules vary by country and depend on the actual working relationship—not only what a contract calls it.

For U.S. context, consult the U.S. Department of Labor guidance on worker misclassification. It is not a global classification test. Get qualified advice in each place where the worker will live and work.

How to manage hiring costs without sacrificing quality

  1. Define the target market, core responsibilities, working hours, and compensation before recruiting.
  2. Use a short, structured interview and a relevant, fictional work sample instead of many unfocused interview rounds.
  3. Consider international talent markets based on customer coverage and role fit, not only pay.
  4. Create reusable product documentation, sales playbooks, prospecting standards, and training exercises.
  5. Measure qualified meetings, attendance, accepted opportunities, pipeline, conversion, and progress against reasonable goals—not just email or call volume.

Should you hire an SDR or outsource sales development?

FactorInternal SDROutsourced sales development
ManagementManaged directly by your companyManaged under the provider's arrangement
Product knowledgeCan develop deep internal knowledgeRequires effective onboarding and coordination
RecruitmentYour responsibilityMay be handled by the provider
Cost structureSalary and employment expensesService fees or another agreed commercial arrangement
Control and scalingDirect employee management; scale through recruitmentDepends on contract and provider capacity

An internal SDR can suit a company that wants direct control and long-term team development. Outsourcing may suit a company that wants external support without building every part of sales development in-house. Compare the commercial terms, management effort, and desired control against your sales process and growth plans.

Frequently asked questions about SDR hiring costs

How much does an SDR cost per month?

The cost of hiring a sales representative depends on location, experience, base pay, commission, tools, recruitment, and employment expenses. Calculate the complete cost rather than relying on salary alone.

What is a good SDR salary in 2026?

A competitive salary depends on the local employment market, role responsibilities, experience, and industry. A U.S. benchmark is not a universal international standard; verify current local data.

Do SDRs receive commission?

Many roles include variable pay in addition to base salary. Commission may be linked to qualified meetings, sales-qualified leads, accepted opportunities, or pipeline.

Is it cheaper to hire an SDR internationally?

It can be, but compare recruitment, local employment obligations, working hours, payments, tools, training, and management as well as compensation.

Should SDRs be paid hourly or monthly?

Either may be appropriate depending on the role, employment structure, and local law. Make the arrangement and variable-pay terms clear.

How many SDRs should a company hire?

Consider your addressable market, sales cycle, prospecting capacity, conversion data, and management resources. Understand the economics of one SDR before scaling.

How long does it take a new SDR to become productive?

Ramp time depends on product complexity, experience, training, and market conditions. Set realistic milestones and coach throughout the initial months.

Is hiring a remote SDR worth it?

It can be worthwhile when your sales process is defined, remote management is in place, expectations are clear, and the representative has the tools and support to succeed.
